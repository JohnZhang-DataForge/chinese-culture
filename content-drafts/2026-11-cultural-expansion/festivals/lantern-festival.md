## A night of light

The Lantern Festival (元宵节, Yuánxiāo Jié) falls on the fifteenth day of the first month in the traditional Chinese calendar, the night of the year's first full moon. It is often described as the final major celebration in the Spring Festival period. Customs include lantern displays, riddles and foods made from glutinous rice, but celebrations vary by region and family.

## Lanterns and riddles

Lanterns turn streets and public spaces into places for evening walks and gatherings. Displays range from simple hand-held lanterns to elaborate installations. In some communities, riddles are attached to lanterns for visitors to solve. Traditional motifs may appear alongside contemporary characters, animals and imaginative scenes.

## A taste of the festival

Glutinous rice balls are widely associated with the occasion. In many regions they are called *tangyuan* (汤圆); in some northern traditions, *yuanxiao* (元宵) is also used for a related festival food. Fillings and preparation methods differ. The round shape is often associated with reunion, though not every family follows the same food custom.

Historical accounts of the festival's development are varied. The fifteenth day of the first lunar month was an important occasion in early imperial China, while lantern-viewing practices developed over time. It is more accurate to understand the festival as a tradition shaped across periods than to present one popular legend as a proven origin.
