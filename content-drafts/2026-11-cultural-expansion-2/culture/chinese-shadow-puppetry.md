## A theatre made of light and movement

Chinese shadow puppetry uses figures made from materials such as leather or paper. Puppeteers move them with rods behind a translucent screen, while a light source projects their silhouettes for an audience. Music and singing accompany the action, turning a small screen into a stage for stories, characters and imagined places.

UNESCO inscribed Chinese shadow puppetry on its Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2011. The tradition includes many local styles, repertoires and ways of making and manipulating figures.

## Making the figures move

A puppet may be cut, carved, coloured and assembled from several pieces. Articulated joints allow its head, arms or legs to move. The puppeteer must coordinate rods, timing and position so that the audience can follow the action. Some performers manipulate more than one figure at once.

The screen is not merely a background. Its size, texture and lighting shape what the audience sees. A figure can appear to enter, leave, travel or transform through a few carefully timed movements.

## Music, voice and shared knowledge

Performances combine movement with singing, speech and instrumental music. In some traditions, performers learn a large repertoire of stories and develop distinctive vocal or musical skills. Knowledge is passed through families, troupes and teacher-student relationships. The people who carve the puppets may also perform with them.

Shadow plays have appeared in entertainment and in community or ceremonial settings. Their subjects and functions differ across regions; no single story or performance style represents the whole tradition.

## Watch for the craft

When watching a short performance, notice how a small change in the puppet's angle alters its expression. Listen for the way music marks a change of mood or action. The illusion is simple in appearance, but it depends on precise coordination among light, screen, voice and hands.
