## A seasonal day of remembrance

Qingming (清明, Qīngmíng) is both one of the traditional twenty-four solar terms and a day associated with remembering ancestors. It usually falls in early April, but its Gregorian date changes from year to year. The name is often translated as “Clear and Bright,” evoking the season's fresh light and renewed landscape.

For many families, Qingming is a time to visit ancestral graves, tidy burial places and make offerings or spend a quiet moment in remembrance. It is also associated with spring outings, when people enjoy the warmer weather and the changing landscape.

## Remembering and caring for family history

Practices vary among regions, communities and families. Some people clean graves, place flowers or offer food; others observe the day in different ways or remember relatives from a distance. The form of remembrance may be shaped by family history, local custom, religious practice and personal choice.

The important point is not that every family performs the same ritual. Qingming provides a seasonal occasion for many people to reflect on family relationships, memory and continuity between generations.

## Spring outdoors

Qingming is also linked with enjoying the outdoors. Families may walk in parks, visit the countryside or fly kites. Some local customs include using willow branches, but these practices are not universal. The festival's seasonal character connects remembrance with the renewal of plants and the arrival of spring.

## A thoughtful way to learn

If you are learning about Qingming, distinguish the solar term from the many customs associated with the day. Ask how a practice varies across regions and generations, and avoid assuming that one family's observance represents everyone.
