## What is Spring Festival?

**Spring Festival (春节 / 春節, Chūnjié)** marks the start of the traditional Chinese new year. It is associated with family reunions, preparations at home, visits, well-wishing and public celebrations. Specific practices vary by region, community and generation.

Because its date follows a traditional lunisolar calendar, the Gregorian date changes each year.

## How do people prepare?

Examples of customs include cleaning and decorating the home, preparing festive dishes, gathering for a reunion meal, and greeting relatives and friends. Red envelopes are often associated with blessings and good wishes, especially across generations.

Some people attend temple fairs or watch public performances. Others celebrate quietly, work through the holiday or take part from abroad. The range of experiences is wide.

## Where do the stories come from?

Traditional tales involving the mythical creature **Nian** are frequently connected with New Year customs. They are an important part of festival storytelling, but should not be treated as a single proven explanation for every historical practice.

## An invitation to observe

Choose one custom and ask a family member, friend or community group what it means to them today. Their answer may differ from a museum exhibit or a textbook—and that difference matters.
