## Why “read” a painting?

A Chinese painting may ask you to move slowly through a landscape, notice a poem, study a seal, or compare a painter's line with calligraphy. Some works were mounted as **handscrolls**, meant to be viewed section by section rather than all at once.

Artistic practices varied enormously across historical periods, regions and patrons. Ink painting is important, but colorful paintings and decorative work are also significant parts of the story.

## Look for three layers

- **The image:** A mountain, plant, person or animal may be carefully depicted, suggested with a few marks, or placed in a large open space.
- **The writing:** Poems and inscriptions can add a voice or comment on a scene.
- **The object's journey:** Seals, collectors' marks and later inscriptions sometimes reveal how an artwork has been viewed across centuries.

## Try a slow-viewing exercise

Choose one painted scroll from a museum collection. Start at one edge and move across deliberately. What appears first? What is left unstated? Does a written passage change your interpretation?
