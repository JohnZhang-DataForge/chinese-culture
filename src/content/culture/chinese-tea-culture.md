## More than a drink

Tea is both an agricultural product and a way people spend time together. A cup may accompany a quiet pause, a conversation with a guest, a family visit or a formal occasion. China's tea traditions include tending tea gardens, picking leaves, processing them, preparing a drink and sharing it. In 2022, UNESCO inscribed traditional Chinese tea processing techniques and associated social practices on its Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.

## Six broad categories

Chinese tea is commonly grouped by processing into green, yellow, dark, white, oolong and black tea. The English term *black tea* can be confusing: this category is called *hongcha* (红茶, “red tea”) in Chinese. It is different from *heicha* (黑茶), usually translated as dark or post-fermented tea. Growing conditions, plant varieties, harvest seasons and processing influence aroma, colour and taste.

## Craft and connection

Tea makers learn through practice, family traditions and apprenticeships. Tea is served at home, at work, in teahouses, restaurants and temples. Offering tea can welcome a visitor or create time to talk, although no single ritual is followed by every household.

Try comparing two teas: notice the dry leaves, aroma, colour and aftertaste. Ask where each was grown and how it was processed.
