## What do we mean by culture?

Culture includes the stories people tell, the languages they speak, the objects they make, and the ways they gather, celebrate, remember and adapt.

China spans many landscapes and communities. What feels familiar in one household may be entirely different in another. A family tradition in the north may not look like one in the south; a festival celebrated abroad may have changed in a new setting. **Variation is not a flaw in the story. It is part of the story.**

The Chinese word **文化 (wénhuà)** is often translated as “culture.” It offers a useful starting point, but no single term can contain all the experiences of people living in China and across Chinese-speaking communities worldwide.

## Three useful questions

When you encounter a cultural practice, ask:

1. **Where and among whom?** A custom may have particular regional, linguistic or family histories.
2. **When?** Traditions change, sometimes quickly, and older accounts may not describe present practice.
3. **What kind of account is this?** A legend can teach us what a community values without being a historical record.

A dragon in a festival parade, for example, can be part of a lively performance, an inherited symbol and a local community event at the same time.

## Your first observation

Choose one custom that matters in your own family or community. Who taught it to you? Has it changed since their childhood?

Now keep that same curiosity when you read about another culture.
