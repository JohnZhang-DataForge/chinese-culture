## Stories travel

A story can be retold through a written book, a stage performance, a film, a children's adaptation or a family conversation. Each retelling can emphasize different values and characters.

Take **Journey to the West (西游记 / 西遊記)**. It blends a fictional pilgrimage with folklore, adventure, satire and religious ideas. Many readers know the Monkey King from modern adaptations long before they encounter the longer novel.

## A few questions for every book

- **Who produced this version?** An original work and a modern translation are not identical.
- **When was it written or compiled?** Historical context changes the meaning of familiar words.
- **What is being claimed?** A mythical journey should not be mistaken for a documentary record.
- **What is missing?** A short children's adaptation may omit much of an older text.

The same care applies to the **Analects (论语 / 論語)**, a collection associated with Confucius and later transmission. Readers benefit from learning how it was compiled rather than imagining it as a single modern book written directly by one author.

## A small exercise

Compare the description of a classic book with an adaptation you know. Which characters or themes receive the most attention?
