## More than a tool

A brush can be used for writing or painting, and the movement of the hand is visible in the line it leaves behind. The expression **笔墨 / 筆墨 (bǐmò)** refers literally to brush and ink but is also used in conversations about technique and artistic character.

Calligraphy is not merely handwriting made fancy. It is a visual practice involving rhythm, spacing, direction, pressure and deliberate variation.

## Look closely

When studying a piece of calligraphy or ink painting, notice:

- Where lines are thick, thin, quick or slow.
- Where the paper is left empty.
- How the eye moves through the composition.
- Whether words, seals or poems appear alongside painted images.

Chinese painting has included richly colored works as well as ink-based paintings. Avoid assuming that a single minimalist style stands for the entire tradition.

## A small exercise

Draw a line slowly with a brush or pen, then draw one quickly. Ask what the marks communicate. Next, find a museum image of a handscroll and look for changes in density and motion.
