# Editorial Strategy and Voice

**Status:** Proposed

## Content promise

Help readers understand Chinese culture with clear definitions, evidence-based background, practical examples and respectful regional context. Prefer original explanation over copying traditional or modern sources.

## Editorial voice

Friendly, curious, calm and precise. Avoid marketing superlatives, claims that one custom represents all Chinese people, unsupported origin stories and political advocacy.

## Content formats

- **Explainer:** one cultural concept answered with history, variations and contemporary relevance.
- **Lesson:** an explicit learning outcome, key concepts and next step.
- **Festival reference:** evergreen background separated from event logistics.
- **Reading guide:** background about a book without unauthorized full-text reproduction.
- **Event announcement:** verifiable practical details with expiry date.

## Research standard

Every nontrivial historical or cultural claim should have a traceable source. Prioritize museums, libraries, scholars, historical sources and reputable cultural institutions. When sources disagree, disclose uncertainty and describe perspectives fairly.

## Editorial review checklist

- Reader question answered within the first section.
- All unfamiliar Chinese terms translated and, when helpful, romanized.
- Tradition, history, folklore and contemporary practice distinguished.
- Sources, quotations and images checked for accuracy and permission.
- Grammar, inclusivity, links, metadata and accessibility reviewed.
- Author or editor, review date and related material recorded.

## Publication rhythm

Start with a realistic pace (for example, one reviewed article per week) rather than committing to large unverified volumes. Review existing content periodically.

## Corrections

Accept feedback, verify it, update text with a meaningful commit and show major substantive corrections where relevant. High-impact inaccuracies should be corrected promptly.
