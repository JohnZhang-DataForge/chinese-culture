# Simplified Chinese Localization Style Guide

**Status:** Working editorial guide for Agent 3; subject to the shared bilingual content contract and Agent 0's final editorial decisions.  
**Locale:** Simplified Chinese (`zh-Hans`)  
**Audience:** Native and fluent Simplified Chinese readers, including families and international learners.

## Voice and tone

- Write natural, contemporary Simplified Chinese. Avoid word-for-word English syntax and generic machine-translation phrasing.
- Keep the existing English editorial voice: welcoming, curious, calm, precise and respectful.
- Prefer clear explanations over promotional language or grand claims.
- Do not assume one custom, region, family practice or historical interpretation represents all Chinese people.
- Keep the original article's structure and teaching intent. Do not shorten or remove meaningful examples, caveats, questions, references or learning exercises.
- Translate the English source's claims; do not add historical details, dates, etymologies or cultural interpretations that the source does not support.
- If the English source is ambiguous or may overstate a fact, flag it for editorial review rather than silently strengthening or weakening the claim.

## Names, Pinyin and typography

1. Use Simplified Chinese for the Chinese-language edition.
2. Use Chinese book-title brackets `《》` for named books and literary works.
3. Include tone-mark Pinyin on first introduction of an educational term or name when it helps pronunciation. Do not add Pinyin mechanically to every repeated occurrence.
4. Preserve the English article's Chinese-character examples, including traditional forms when the comparison is the point (for example, `书 / 書`).
5. Keep personal and place names consistent across the full site. Do not invent Chinese names for people or institutions that the source does not identify.
6. Use Chinese punctuation in Chinese prose. Retain official organization names and source URLs as published.
7. Keep dates and calendar explanations faithful to the source. Do not add a Gregorian festival date unless a specific year and authoritative source are supplied.

## Cultural and historical accuracy

- Distinguish historical evidence, traditional narrative, folklore, contemporary practice and editorial interpretation.
- Preserve explicit uncertainty markers such as “one widely told tradition”, “in some communities”, “may”, “often” and “not every family”.
- Avoid converting a legend into a factual origin story.
- Keep regional, generational, family, religious and diaspora variation visible.
- Do not present Mandarin as the only Chinese language variety.
- Do not imply that simplified and traditional character forms map neatly to separate identities or that one form is universal.
- Do not turn an evergreen festival guide into an announcement for a particular year's event.
- Preserve source attribution and further-reading links. Do not translate or fabricate source titles in a way that makes the linked source harder to identify.

## Translation review checklist

- [ ] Every English paragraph, heading, list item, question, callout and caption has a meaningful Chinese counterpart.
- [ ] The Chinese text reads naturally when read without the English source.
- [ ] No historical or cultural claim has been added, removed or strengthened without evidence.
- [ ] Regional variation and uncertainty are preserved.
- [ ] Names, Chinese characters, Pinyin and book titles are consistent.
- [ ] Dates, quantities and factual details match the English source.
- [ ] Internal links and source links remain valid.
- [ ] Chinese title, description and SEO metadata are complete.
- [ ] No placeholder, untranslated English sentence or machine-translation note remains.
- [ ] Ambiguous or unsupported claims are listed for editorial review.

## Scope boundary

This guide records translation conventions only. It does not define frontmatter, file layout, content IDs, localized routes, publication-state semantics or the shared data schema. Those must follow Agent 0's approved contract and Agent 2's route/localization agreement.
