# Security, Privacy, Copyright and Responsible Publishing

**Status:** Proposed; not legal advice.

## Security baseline

Keep GitHub private with least-privilege collaborators, protected main branch where possible, account MFA, reviewed changes and restricted deployment credentials. Publish static files only; avoid exposing internal documentation or secrets in public assets.

## Privacy baseline

Do not add accounts, contact forms or personal-data analytics at launch by default. If a contact mechanism is used, disclose where messages go and how long they are retained. Add a privacy policy that reflects the actual deployed services and jurisdictional requirements.

## Copyright

Use original writing and content for which adequate permissions exist. Verify image licenses, attribution and any modification requirements. Do not assume ancient original works make modern translations, illustrations or editions free to distribute.

## Cultural responsibility

Distinguish legend from documented history, avoid essentializing a diverse population, describe cultural variation and seek qualified review where sources or traditions are contested.

## Outbound links and events

Check organizer legitimacy and avoid presenting third-party promotions as endorsements. Clearly state when an event is independent of the site.

## Advertising/sponsorship

If introduced later, mark paid placements and affiliate relationships clearly, preserve editorial independence and avoid behavioral tracking by default.

## Prelaunch approvals

Verify policies with relevant specialists where needed, confirm data-processing and cookie behavior, and document takedown/correction contact channels. No generic boilerplate should claim protections or compliance not actually implemented.
